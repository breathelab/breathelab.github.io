Laboratory of Bioengineered Respiratory Emulation Approaches for Tuberculosis and Health

We develop cutting-edge human in vitro lung models using human induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSC) to understand how Mycobacterium tuberculosis interacts with the lung innate immunity, and what determines the success or failure of anitmicrobial responses and tissue homeostasis.

Our research Contact us

Bacteria

How Mycobacterium tuberculosis establishes itself in the alveolus and subverts early innate immune responses.

Cell

iPSC-derived alveolar epithelium, macrophages and endothelium, giving a defined and donor-matched cellular system.

Device

Microfluidic organ-on-chip platforms that reconstruct the air–liquid interface, perfusion and mechanical cues of the lung.

Patients

Findings anchored to TB cohorts in East London, linking in vitro mechanism to patient outcomes.

Research

Our work sits at the interface of infection microbiology and bioengineering. We believe the development of physiologically relevant and fit for purpose complex in vitro lung models will enable us to better emulate and understand the host-TB interactions and TB pathogenesis.
